The best net worth tracker depends on what your balance sheet is made of. If it is a brokerage account, a retirement plan, a bank account and a mortgage, Empower's free dashboard links all of it and charts the total. If a large share sits in crypto, private stock, fund commitments or property, Kubera at $250 a year is the only app on this list that tracks those with the same care as a brokerage balance. If the investment accounts are the part you want to understand rather than just total up, that is a portfolio tool's job, and Helm Terminal is the one I build. Prices below were checked against each vendor's own pages in September 2026.

Net worth is assets minus liabilities. Every tool here computes that. Where they differ is which assets they can see without you typing them in, how the numbers get refreshed, and what the app was built to do once it has the total. This post covers eight options across those three questions, plus one I left out because its price could not be verified.

## 1. Empower Personal Dashboard

**Price:** free. Empower's [tools page](https://www.empower.com/tools) answers "what does it cost" with "Nothing! The dashboard experience and financial tools are completely free and take just a few minutes to set up."

**What it aggregates:** the same page lists "IRAs, 401(k) and other retirement plans, investments, mortgages, loans, savings and checking accounts, credit cards, and more." That is the widest free coverage of ordinary accounts on this list, and it includes the liability side, which many investment-first tools skip.

**Sync method:** automatic account linking. The page does not name the aggregator.

**Built for:** a net worth tracker and a retirement planner, both free, sitting in front of an advisory business. Empower's [personal investors page](https://www.empower.com/personal-investors) states that Personal Strategy advisory services "are available when you invest $100,000 or more with us" and Private Client from $1,000,000, with fees "calculated based upon the amount of assets being managed."

**What it lacks:** crypto wallets, real estate valuation and manual asset entry are not mentioned on the tools page, so the dashboard is at its best when the balance sheet is made of linkable accounts. Investment accounts appear as balances and allocation rather than holdings-level risk. And the free dashboard exists to introduce the paid advisory service, which is the trade Empower is making. Our [Empower alternatives](/blog/best-personal-capital-alternatives) post covers who tends to leave and where they go.

## 2. Kubera

**Price:** [Kubera's site](https://www.kubera.com/) lists Essentials at $250/year and Black at $2,500/year, each with a 14-day trial. Black adds ownership tags, nested portfolios, guided onboarding and VIP support.

**What it aggregates:** "Thousands of banks and brokerages worldwide," stocks on "every major stock exchange in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, and Asia," crypto across "major blockchains, exchanges, and wallets" including DeFi, NFTs, staking and lending, LP positions with "committed capital, calls, distributions, and IRR," and real estate, vehicles, jewelry and watches under the line "If you can own it, Kubera can track it." Stablecoins count as cash.

**Sync method:** automatic linking for banks, brokerages and crypto, with manual valuations for private assets.

**Built for:** the full balance sheet of someone whose wealth does not fit in a brokerage account. Multi-currency, private stock and fund commitments are the reasons to pay $250 a year.

**What it lacks:** budgeting and cash flow are absent, and Kubera tracks the liquid portion without analyzing it. It reports what a brokerage account is worth, not single-name concentration after looking through ETFs or harvestable losses net of wash-sale windows. The [Kubera alternatives](/blog/best-kubera-alternatives) post goes deeper on when the breadth is worth the price.

## 3. Copilot Money

**Price:** [copilot.money](https://copilot.money/) lists $7.92 a month billed yearly at $95, with a one-month free trial and "No ads, no hidden fees." A monthly billing option exists but the monthly price is not printed on the pricing page.

**What it aggregates:** "All of your accounts, spending, and investments, automatically tracked," summarized on the homepage as "Stocks, crypto, your house." Crypto comes through a Coinbase connection. Home value works by address: "Type in your address. We'll track what your place is worth."

**Sync method:** automatic linking, with the option to "choose how manual you'd like it to be."

**Built for:** a daily personal finance app on iPhone, iPad, Mac and web, with spending categorization, budgets, subscription detection and a net worth screen.

**What it lacks:** Android. Investment tracking shows live performance for stocks and ETFs rather than allocation or risk analysis, and there is no private asset or LP support.

## 4. Quicken Simplifi

**Price:** the [Simplifi product page](https://www.quicken.com/products/simplifi/) prints "$3.99 $6.99 /month Billed annually," meaning $3.99 introductory and $6.99 regular, both billed as a year up front. No trial is mentioned on that page.

**What it aggregates:** "banking, investments & more" across "over 14k financial institutions." Investments get cost basis, performance analysis and real-time quotes.

**Sync method:** automatic linking.

**Built for:** budgeting, bills and savings goals with account balances rolled into a total. It is the cheapest paid app on this list.

**What it lacks:** the product page does not mention net worth by name, real estate or crypto. Investment accounts contribute a balance and basic performance rather than exposure analysis. Our [net worth tracking guide](/blog/how-to-track-net-worth) explains why that distinction matters once most of the total sits in investments.

## 5. YNAB

**Price:** [YNAB's pricing page](https://www.ynab.com/pricing) lists $14.99 a month or $109 paid annually, which it works out to $9.08 a month, with a 34-day free trial and no credit card required when signing up directly.

**What it aggregates:** bank accounts by direct import, which "currently supports select US, Canadian, UK, and EU Banks." Any other account can be added as a tracking balance and updated by hand.

**Sync method:** direct import where supported, manual elsewhere.

**Built for:** zero-based budgeting. Every dollar gets a job, debt payoff and savings goals are tracked closely, and the net worth figure is a by-product of the accounts you add.

**What it lacks:** the pricing page does not mention investment tracking or net worth reporting at all. Investment accounts are balances, not holdings, so two portfolios of equal value read identically whether one is a single stock and the other is a diversified book. YNAB is the most expensive budgeting app here and the least investment-aware.

## 6. Tiller

**Price:** [Tiller's pricing page](https://tiller.com/pricing/) states "After 30 days, Tiller is just $99/year, never any ads or hidden fees." The trial is 30 days.

**What it aggregates:** bank and brokerage balances and transactions, fed into a spreadsheet. The [homepage](https://tiller.com/) describes the Foundation Template as tracking "budgets, expenses, debt, net worth, savings, and more," and quotes Morningstar on its "solid budgeting and net worth templates."

**Sync method:** automatic feeds into Google Sheets or Excel. You own the spreadsheet; Tiller fills it.

**Built for:** people who already run their finances in a spreadsheet and want the data entry automated without giving up the formulas. Net worth is one tab among several.

**What it lacks:** everything beyond the feed is yours to build. There is no app-level analysis, no real estate valuation feed and no crypto wallet connection mentioned. Brokerage feeds deliver balances into cells; holdings analysis is whatever you write.

## 7. Google Sheets template

**Price:** free.

**What it aggregates:** anything you type. One row per account, a balance column, a date column, assets positive and liabilities negative, summed to a total. For listed tickers, the [GOOGLEFINANCE](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/3093281) function pulls quotes so holdings reprice without retyping. It does not reach brokerage balances, retirement accounts or loans, so those stay manual.

**Sync method:** manual, monthly is the cadence most people sustain.

**Built for:** total control at zero cost. No third party sees the data, every category is yours, and adding a dated column each month turns the sheet into a chart.

**What it lacks:** the update discipline. Manual sheets stay accurate exactly as long as they get updated, and the [how-to guide](/blog/how-to-track-net-worth) covers the template and its failure mode in detail.

## How the eight sort themselves

**By what they can see.** Kubera sees the most: worldwide accounts, crypto, private holdings, property. Empower and Copilot cover ordinary linked accounts and, in Copilot's case, home value by address. Simplifi and YNAB are bank-first with investments as balances. Tiller and a Google Sheet see whatever the feed or your fingers put in the cells. Helm sees brokerage and bank accounts and goes deepest on the brokerage side.

**By how they refresh.** Six of the eight link accounts automatically. YNAB links select banks and leaves the rest manual. The Google Sheet is entirely manual, and GOOGLEFINANCE only reprices listed tickers you have already entered.

**By what they do with the total.** Budgeting apps (Simplifi, YNAB, Copilot) use net worth as a scoreboard next to spending. Balance sheet apps (Empower, Kubera, Tiller, the Sheet) make the total the product. Helm treats the investment accounts underneath the total as the thing to work on: concentration, earnings, harvestable losses.

## Where workplace retirement plans fit

A 401(k) or 403(b) is often the largest single line on a household balance sheet and the hardest to link. Coverage depends on the plan's recordkeeper rather than the app, and some recordkeepers restrict third-party access entirely. Empower's tools page lists 401(k) plans among the accounts it connects. For the other apps, a plan that will not link goes in as a manual balance, which every tool here allows. The [401(k), IRA and brokerage in one place](/blog/track-401k-ira-brokerage-one-place) post walks through the linking problem account type by account type.

## A note on safety

Token-based aggregators such as Plaid pass credentials to the institution, return a revocable read-only token, and do not hold the password. Read-only means no trades and no transfers. Where a vendor page does not name its aggregator, the mechanism is the open question before linking a brokerage.
