Atom Finance was a good product built on a defensible idea. Eric Shoykhet started it in Brooklyn in 2018 with the argument that the research stack available to a hedge fund analyst was not that expensive to build, and that the reason retail investors did not have it was pricing convention rather than physics. Estimates, transcripts, comps, models, a clean mobile interface, mostly free. For a while it was one of the more interesting things happening in consumer fintech.

Then in May 2024 Reflexivity, formerly Toggle AI, acquired it. Reflexivity sells to professionals. The consumer terminal was not carried forward, and users reported it going dark around that time. As of August 2026 the domain redirects and I could not reach a working consumer product. I have not found a formal sunset notice, so if you still have a login, take this as a map rather than an obituary.

Either way, the reason you are reading this is that some specific thing Atom did for you stopped happening. So let me split it by the thing.

## Koyfin, if you miss the terminal

Koyfin is the closest match in spirit. Build your own dashboards, look up any company, chart fundamentals over time, get analyst estimates and macro data in the same place. It lists a free tier, Plus at $39 a month and Premium at $79 a month, with advisor tiers above that.

Good at: coverage and layout. The free tier is unusually generous, and Plus covers most of what a serious individual investor needs, including ten years of financials and estimates.

Stops at: your own portfolio. Koyfin will tell you everything about a company and nothing about your position in it. Its screener is also fine rather than excellent, so if screening was your main use, look at Finviz or Stock Rover instead.

## Fiscal.ai, if you miss asking questions

Formerly FinChat. You type a question about a company in plain language and it answers against the filings, including segment level detail that most consumer products flatten into a single revenue line.

Good at: the specific KPIs nobody else breaks out, and follow-up questions. If you used Atom to answer "how has this segment's margin moved since 2021," this is where that question goes now.

Stops at: it is oriented around companies, not around your holdings, and the free tier caps how many questions you get. As of August 2026, check fiscal.ai for current plan pricing.

## TIKR, if you miss estimates and transcripts

TIKR lists a free tier covering US stocks, Plus at $24.95 a month, Pro at $54.95 and Ultimate at $119.95, with annual billing available.

Good at: consensus forecasts, long financial history and earnings call transcripts with real archive depth. This is the closest thing to the part of Atom that felt like it had institutional data behind it.

Stops at: interface polish and screening. It is a data product first.

## Public.com Alpha, if you want filings-aware AI for free

Worth saying plainly, because it is a genuine strength: Public ships an AI research product called Alpha that is free to anyone with a Public account. Public's own documentation says it draws on SEC filings, earnings calls and transcripts, quotes and historicals, analyst ratings and reports, news and social sentiment, and that it is "available to all Public investors at no charge."

Good at: cost, obviously, and the fact that it is grounded in filings rather than only in general web text. For a lot of former Atom users this is the single highest value swap available.

Stops at: it lives inside a brokerage, so it is best if Public is where your money already is, and Public describes Alpha as experimental technology that may give inaccurate responses and explicitly not investment research. That is an honest disclaimer and you should read it as one.

## Seeking Alpha, if you miss the discussion

Atom had a community layer. If what you actually valued was reading other people's arguments about a company, Seeking Alpha is the largest version of that, with thousands of contributors and a quant grading system layered on top.

Good at: volume and contrarian takes. Somebody has written the bear case on your position, and reading it is usually worth twenty minutes.

Stops at: quality varies enormously by author, and the grading system can lend a false air of objectivity to what is still opinion. As of August 2026, check seekingalpha.com for current Premium pricing.

## Reflexivity, if you want the acquirer

The company that bought Atom is still operating and still building AI-driven market analytics. It is aimed at professional and institutional users, and priced accordingly, so for most individual investors it is a curiosity rather than a replacement. Worth knowing it exists, since some of the engineering you liked went there.

## Helm Terminal, and what it actually is

I built this, so weigh it accordingly, and let me be direct about something first: Helm is not a rebuilt Atom. Atom was a terminal for looking up companies. If that is what you miss, Koyfin or Fiscal.ai is your answer and you should stop reading here.

Helm does a different job. It connects every brokerage account you have through Plaid, records the reasons you own each position, then reads SEC filings and market reporting against those reasons continuously and tells you when the evidence turns against one. When it flags something it quotes the source document verbatim, with its date and a link, so you are checking a primary source rather than trusting a score. It also computes tax-loss harvesting across all your accounts at once, which no single brokerage can do, because a broker only sees the money it holds.

Free tier: the full terminal, brokerage sync, AI analysis on any US ticker, and one thesis with twelve months of evidence. Pro is $20 a month after a 14 day trial that requires a card, and it watches every position you own.

Stops at: US listed securities, no screener, and no comps modeling. Helm is not a registered investment adviser and nothing in it is investment advice.

## Rebuilding what Atom did

If you were a heavy Atom user, the honest replacement is two products, not one, and that is a little annoying but it is the truth. Koyfin free plus a Public account gets you a capable terminal and filings-aware AI for nothing, which is roughly where Atom sat on price. Add TIKR or Fiscal.ai if estimates and transcripts were the reason you logged in.

The larger lesson from Atom, and I say this as someone building in the same neighborhood, is that a research terminal you have to remember to open is fragile. It competes with your attention, and attention is the one resource retail investors reliably run out of. The thing worth having is whatever keeps reading when you have stopped looking.
