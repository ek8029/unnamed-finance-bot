Stock Rover is one of the deepest fundamental screeners sold to individual investors, and the honest short verdict is that it earns the price for people who screen often, want 10 to 20 years of financial history, and are comfortable driving a dense grid. It is a research tool first. The portfolio side is real but built around nightly imports, the charting is functional rather than a trading surface, there is no native mobile app, and coverage stops at the US and Canada. Everything below was checked against stockrover.com in September 2026.

If the question is whether to leave Stock Rover rather than whether to start, the [Stock Rover alternatives](/blog/best-stock-rover-alternatives) post covers that ground. This one is about the product itself.

## What Stock Rover is

Stock Rover is a browser-based research platform covering US and Canadian stocks, ETFs and mutual funds. The plans page lists over 8,500 stocks, 7,000+ ETFs and 40,000+ funds; the plan comparison page says 14,000+ North American stocks, so the stock count depends on which page you read. The product has four jobs: screening, research reports and ratings, portfolio analytics, and charting. It does them in roughly that order of strength.

## Who it is built for

The plan names describe the buyer: Premium is "For the Independent Investor," Premium Plus "For the Tactical Investor," Ultimate "For the Wealth Builder & Retiree," and Ultimate Pro "For Advisors & Wealth Professionals."

In practice the fit is a self-directed investor who picks individual stocks on fundamentals, compares candidates against long history, and does not mind a spreadsheet-like interface. Stock Rover's own 2026 self-review page names the learning curve directly: "the power shows up as density." The same page states there are "no earnings-call transcripts" and "no human-written analysis: the reports are data-generated," so anyone who wants a written opinion or a chart-first workflow is not the target.

## The screener

This is the reason to pay. The metric library scales by tier: 400+ financial metrics on Premium, 700+ on Premium Plus, 800+ on Ultimate and Ultimate Pro. Historical fundamentals run 5 years on Premium, 10 on Premium Plus, and 20 on Ultimate and above. Price history is 10 years on Premium and 20 years on the rest. Dividend history is 20 years on the lower two plans and 30 years on the upper two.

What separates the tiers is what you can do with the metrics. Per the comparison page, equation-based screening, screening on historical data, custom metrics, tiled comparative views and 180+ ETF screening metrics start at Premium Plus. Percentile and OTC screening start at Ultimate. Custom metrics run 0, 30 and unlimited across Premium, Premium Plus and Ultimate. Saved screeners run 15, 30, 120 and 1,000 across the four plans, and table rows returned run 250, 500, 1,000 and 5,000. Ranked screening, which turns a pass/fail filter into a sorted list, is on every paid plan.

The practical reading: Premium is a screener with a ceiling, and the ceiling is the first thing an active user hits. Historical screening, which asks "which companies had rising margins in each of the last five years" instead of "which companies have high margins today," is the feature that separates Stock Rover from a free screener, and it is not on the entry plan.

## Research reports and Stock Rover ratings

Stock Rover generates an 8-page research report on demand for 7,000+ North American stocks. The reports page lists the contents: an overall score, an intrinsic value estimate with margin of safety, fundamental strength across value, growth, quality and sentiment, 5-year financial statement summaries, peer rankings graded 1 to 100, competitor comparison, dividend sustainability, analyst price targets, and automated risk flags for things like deteriorating cash flow or rising debt.

Reports are included from Premium Plus up and are a +$50 add-on on Premium. ETF and fund reports, covering 7,400 ETFs and 20,000 funds, are full on Ultimate and Ultimate Pro and "sample" on the two lower plans.

The ratings behind those reports score six categories: Growth, Valuation, Efficiency, Financial Strength, Dividends and Momentum. The help documentation describes analyzing "the trajectory and volatility of multiple metrics within each category over time," with each metric's raw rating "then converted into an industry group percentile rank." The formulas are proprietary. Two things follow. Every rating is relative to industry peers, so a 90 in a weak industry is not a 90 in a strong one. And a percentile is a summary of a model whose weighting the report never shows.

On Premium, stock ratings are capped at 10 per month plus the Dow 30, and fair value, stock scores and investor warnings at 20 tickers per month plus the Dow 30. Premium Plus and above are unlimited. Real-time quotes, analyst detail, option metrics and insider metrics are Ultimate and above.

## Portfolio tools, and what "brokerage connection" means here

Every paid plan includes portfolio management, brokerage integration, rebalancing, a Trade Evaluator, future dividend income projections, Monte Carlo simulation and correlation analysis. The portfolio management page describes IRR and time-weighted returns side by side, risk versus the S&P 500, and Sharpe ratio, beta, volatility and drawdown. Rebalancing compares target versus actual allocation and calculates the trades to restore it.

The brokerage part deserves a precise description because the phrase means different things on different products. Stock Rover connects through Yodlee, a third-party aggregator, so brokerage credentials go to Yodlee rather than Stock Rover. The help pages state that linked portfolios "are updated nightly and can also be refreshed manually at any time." Interactive Brokers is handled through "an alternate direct feed" arranged by emailing support. Connection limits by plan are 2, 5, 15 and 60, and Free accounts are limited to two after the trial.

So this is an import of positions and balances, refreshed once a day, feeding a portfolio that Stock Rover then analyzes. It is not a live stream, and the help pages do not describe transactions, cost basis or tax lots as part of what comes over. The portfolio management page makes no mention of tax-loss harvesting or cost basis. It analyzes what you hold against fundamentals; it does not do tax work.

## Charting

Charting is the weak point, and Stock Rover says so itself: "Charting is utilitarian: built for research, not trading." The charting page lists 20 years of price history, candlestick and OHLC charts, log scale, ratio charts (one ticker divided by another, such as MSFT / SPY), 50 and 200 day moving averages, RSI and Bollinger Bands, and fundamental charting of revenue, net income, free cash flow, dividends and ratios over the same 20 years. Valuation and ratio charts start at Premium Plus; drawing tools start at Ultimate.

The same page lists what it does not do: intraday charting, multiple simultaneous charts, and tabular export of chart data, the last described as on the long-term roadmap. Anyone who trades off a chart will keep a separate charting tool.

## Alerts

Email alerts are on every paid plan, and the plans page also lists text alerting. The alerts help page lists triggers for price thresholds, moving average crosses, 52-week highs and lows, valuation metrics such as P/E or P/S, dividend yield, upcoming earnings and unusual volume, set on individual tickers, indices, or entire portfolios and watchlists at once.

## Where it falls short

**The tier ladder is steep and the useful features climb it.** Historical screening, custom metrics, equation screening and included research reports start at Premium Plus; 20-year fundamentals, real-time quotes and drawing tools start at Ultimate. The plan most people would call "the real Stock Rover" is $948 a year.

**Nothing watches for you.** A screen is something you run. The alerts cover price, a handful of metrics and earnings dates, but nothing reads a filing against the reason you bought a position. Stock Rover finds candidates. It does not monitor a thesis.

**Coverage stops at the border.** US and Canada only. Stock Rover's own materials state "no global exchanges, FX or macro terminals."

**No native mobile app.** The mobile web interface covers the dashboard, ticker search and basic list access. The full grid is a desktop product.

**Data freshness varies.** The FAQ states most quotes are real-time, but obscure stocks are delayed about 5 minutes and Canadian and micro-cap stocks about 15 minutes, and the comparison page lists real-time quotes as an Ultimate and above feature.

**No transcripts, no human analysis.** Both stated on Stock Rover's own review page.

## How it compares

**Koyfin** is the closest match in ambition and the opposite in feel: global coverage, dashboards you arrange yourself, analyst estimates and macro data that Stock Rover does not touch, and a screener that is fine rather than deep. The choice is screening depth on North American stocks versus breadth across markets. Current Koyfin pricing is in the [alternatives post](/blog/best-stock-rover-alternatives).

**Finviz** is faster and shallower. The free screener filters the whole US market in seconds and the heat map is still the quickest read on a trading day, but statement history is a fraction of Stock Rover's and there is no comparable portfolio layer. Finviz is where a screen starts; Stock Rover is where a research file gets built.

**Morningstar Investor** is the other philosophy: a written analyst view, a fair value estimate and a moat rating, with Portfolio X-Ray to show overlapping exposure across funds. It is a library to read, not a machine to drive. Someone who owns mostly funds gets more from Morningstar; someone who compares individual stocks on 20 years of numbers gets more from Stock Rover.

## Verdict

Stock Rover fits an investor who screens North American stocks on fundamentals regularly, wants long financial history in a comparison grid, and is at a desk when doing it. The fit is strongest on Premium Plus and Ultimate, where the features that distinguish it from free screeners live. It fits poorly for anyone who needs global coverage, a trading chart, a phone-first workflow, transcripts, or a written analyst opinion, and it does not do tax work or thesis monitoring at all. Premium is a way to learn the interface, with the expectation that the ceiling arrives quickly.
