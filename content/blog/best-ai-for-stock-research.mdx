**The best AI for stock research depends on the work you need done.** Start with Claude for comparing filings, ChatGPT for calculations you can inspect, Perplexity for finding current sources, and Gemini for exploring news and industry context. These are suggested workflows, not a league table of model accuracy.

I build Helm Terminal, so this comparison includes my product and its limits. The question worth asking: **what would make your next investment review better?** A faster summary, a calculation you can reproduce, or a record of whether your original reasons for owning a stock still hold?

## The short answer

| Your research task | A useful starting point | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare a 10-K with an earnings transcript | Claude with the actual documents | Exact passages, dates, and changes in management's explanation |
| Analyze a spreadsheet or build scenarios | ChatGPT with your data | Inputs, formulas, units, and assumptions |
| Find the news behind today's move | Perplexity | Original publication, timestamp, and evidence of causation |
| Explore an industry or macro story | Gemini | Supporting sources and what remains uncertain |
| Receive recurring updates | A supported scheduled task or alert | Schedule, account access, coverage, and notifications |
| Keep holdings, theses, and subsequent evidence together | A portfolio workflow such as Helm | Monitored positions, last check, evidence, history, and plan limits |

Capabilities overlap and change. You do not need four paid subscriptions to start: give one tool a specific job and verify its output before adding another.

## Claude: compare the documents

[Claude](/blog/claude-stock-analysis) can work with uploaded filings and transcripts. Its file tools also cover spreadsheets and code execution, so treating it as only a writing assistant would miss part of the product. [Document support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-upload-files-to-claude), [file creation and analysis](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12111783-create-and-edit-files-with-claude).

Try this with the actual source documents:

> Compare these two earnings transcripts. For each change in guidance, demand, margins, or capital spending, show the earlier statement, the newer statement, reporting dates, and source location. Separate a measurable change from a change in tone. Say when the documents do not establish an answer.

Check the original passages, particularly qualifiers such as "approximately" or "excluding." The useful output is a reviewable comparison; a polished summary alone can hide missing context.

## Perplexity: find the current sources

[Perplexity](/blog/perplexity-stock-research) is a useful starting point for finding news behind a company update. Its Finance product also includes an earnings hub and price alerts; alerts can trigger a query and notification. [Perplexity Finance features](https://www.perplexity.ai/changelog/what-we-shipped-july-18th).

> Find the primary sources behind today's move in [TICKER]. Separate company announcements, SEC filings, and analyst commentary. Give each publication time. If the move cannot be confidently explained, say so rather than inventing a reason.

Follow the links. A recent article can repeat an old number, and a cited document can be misread. The timestamp and reporting period matter as much as the presence of a citation.

## ChatGPT: make the calculations inspectable

[ChatGPT](/blog/chatgpt-stock-analysis) can analyze uploaded spreadsheets, create charts, and run Python for calculations. That makes it useful for scenario analysis when you supply inputs and inspect the method. [OpenAI's data-analysis guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8437071-data-analysis-with-chatgpt).

> Using this spreadsheet, calculate each holding's weight. Show the formula and reconcile the weights to 100%. Model a 10% decline in the three largest positions with all others unchanged. Keep observed data separate from scenario assumptions.

For valuation, make growth assumptions, margins, discount rates, and terminal values explicit. An attractive chart cannot repair a wrong input.

ChatGPT also supports scheduled tasks and, where eligible Finances access is available, portfolio updates. Availability varies. A comparison claiming it can never work between sessions would be inaccurate. [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617).

## Gemini: explore the wider context

[Gemini](/blog/gemini-stock-analysis) can help organize questions around industry developments and surface related sources. Its double-check feature uses Google Search to look for supporting or conflicting material. Google explains that a matching link is not necessarily the source originally used to generate the answer. [Gemini's source and double-check guide](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14143489?hl=en).

> Explain how this industry development could affect [COMPANY]. Separate confirmed facts from hypotheses. Find primary sources for the facts and list the next disclosures that could support or weaken each hypothesis.

That produces a research agenda. It should not turn a plausible industry narrative into a claim about future earnings.

## Research and portfolio monitoring: compare the workflow

AI tools can search, retain context, and run scheduled work. The relevant difference is how well a particular setup supports your investing process.

1. **What does it cover?** Your actual holdings, a watchlist, or a list typed into a prompt?
2. **What is it checking?** A price threshold, general news, or a specific claim in your investment thesis?
3. **When did it last check?** Look for a timestamp and understand the schedule and data coverage.
4. **What changed?** Inspect the evidence and compare it with the earlier view.
5. **What does the plan include?** One monitored position and a whole portfolio are different offers.

For example, "margins must remain above my thesis threshold" is a useful claim to revisit after earnings. Your threshold is an assumption to test, not an AI forecast.

## A repeatable stock-research workflow

Find the primary filing or announcement. Compare it with the previous disclosure. Check numbers using visible inputs and formulas. Record why you own the stock and what would change your mind. Choose how to revisit those reasons: a calendar review, a configured AI task, or a dedicated portfolio tool.

The goal is a decision process you can explain and repeat. No model name, citation badge, or subscription price can substitute for that.
