# Your Portfolio Has a Single Point of Failure

A colleague of mine lost $340,000 in six months.

Not on options. Not on crypto. He held NVIDIA. Blue chip. Best performer of the decade. 67% of his net worth. RSU vests he never sold because the stock only went up.

Then it didn't.

One guidance revision. One sector rotation. Portfolio dropped from $900K to $560K while the S&P fell 8%. That's not a stock picking failure. That's a concentration failure.

He knew he was concentrated. He just never measured how bad it actually was.

---

## This probably applies to you

Concentration sneaks in four ways. I've seen all four in real portfolios this month.

**RSU vests you haven't touched.** Three years of vesting at Google or Meta or NVIDIA and suddenly one stock is 40-60% of everything you own. You meant to diversify. You just didn't get around to it.

**Winners you didn't trim.** That $10K in TSLA is now $45K. Selling your best performer feels wrong. So you hold, and now it's a quarter of your portfolio. You didn't choose a concentrated bet. The market chose it for you.

**The index fund thing.** VTI holds 3,600 stocks. The top 10 are 33% of the fund. Apple alone is 7%. "Total market" is a mega-cap tech bet with a long tail of stocks that barely matter.

**Inherited positions.** Cost basis of $12 from 1985. Selling triggers a tax bomb. So you sit on $800K in one name because the alternative feels worse.

---

## The number

The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index. Same formula the DOJ uses for antitrust. Square each position's weight, sum them up.

Under 0.15: diversified. 0.15 to 0.25: concentrated. Over 0.25: dangerous.

One stock at 45% of your portfolio puts you above 0.23. Concentrated zone. Doesn't matter how many small positions you hold alongside it.

Simpler test: take your biggest position. If it drops 30% tomorrow — normal for an individual stock — how much does your total portfolio drop? More than 5%? That's a problem.

---

## The math that should bother you

$500K portfolio, diversified (HHI 0.08). Severe downturn. Max drawdown: 25%. You lose $125K.

Same $500K, 45% in one stock (HHI 0.23). Max drawdown: 40%+. You lose $200K.

That extra $75K is the concentration tax. You pay it every time the market gets ugly. It doesn't build roads. It just evaporates.

## What to do about it

**See everything in one place.** You've got a brokerage, a 401(k), maybe an IRA. Each looks fine on its own. Combine them and the concentration jumps out. Your brokerage won't show you this because they only see their piece.

**Harvest losses first.** Before trimming winners, sell your losers. The realized losses offset the gains from reducing your concentrated positions. I wrote a detailed walkthrough [here](https://helmterminal.dev/blog/tax-loss-harvesting-guide). There's also a [free calculator](https://helmterminal.dev/tools/tlh-calculator) if you want to estimate the savings before doing anything.

**Set rules.** "If any position hits 25%, trim to 20%." Write it down. Tape it to your monitor. Rules exist because your brain will talk you out of selling your winners every single time.

**Replace, don't just sell.** Trim NVIDIA, buy SMH. You keep semiconductor exposure without the single-stock risk.

**Watch it.** Concentration rebuilds itself. Prices move. The position you trimmed last quarter drifts right back up. You need something monitoring this, not a calendar reminder to check a spreadsheet.
