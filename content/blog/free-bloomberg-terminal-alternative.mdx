**The best free Bloomberg Terminal alternative depends on the job.** Start with TradingView for charts, Finviz for screening, or Koyfin for company fundamentals. If your question is about the stocks you already own, Helm combines a portfolio view with evidence to review against an investment thesis.

You do not need seven subscriptions or a replica of an institutional trading desk. You need a reliable way to answer your next investing question.

## What Does Bloomberg Actually Give You?

[Bloomberg Terminal](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/bloomberg-terminal/) combines financial data, news, analytics and a professional communications network. A free stock screener or charting app covers a narrower job. None of the tools here should be treated as a replacement for all of that infrastructure.

Bloomberg is a subscription product and accepts [demo requests](https://professional.bloomberg.com/request-demo/). Ask Bloomberg for current pricing and terms rather than budgeting from an old annual-price figure in a comparison article.

For an individual investor, a useful starting question is more specific: “What changed in this company?”, “How concentrated is my portfolio?” or “What evidence would change my mind about this stock?” Choose a tool that answers that question before paying for more data.

## The Best Free Bloomberg Terminal Alternatives

### 1. Helm Terminal (Free Tier)

**Best fit: reviewing what you own and why you own it.**

[Helm](/) supports read-only brokerage connections through Plaid and manual holdings entry. Free includes the portfolio dashboard, general daily brief, actions inbox and one investment thesis with up to twelve months of evidence history and ongoing monitoring.

For example, you might own a company because you expect its margins to improve. Save that reason, then review cited evidence that supports or challenges it. Monitoring runs on a schedule; a new finding is not guaranteed each day.

**The limit:** Free monitors one thesis. [Pro](/pricing), at $20 a month or $149 a year, extends monitoring across your tracked theses and adds a tailored brief, earnings exposure and detailed tax tools. Available evidence and portfolio analysis depend on coverage and the data you provide.

You can try [public stock analysis](/analyze) before signup, subject to daily limits, and the free [ETF overlap checker](/tools/etf-overlap) shows where two funds hold the same companies at the top without an account either. Helm focuses on stock research and portfolio context; it does not reproduce Bloomberg's professional communications network or fixed-income tooling.

### 2. Yahoo Finance

**Best fit: a familiar starting point for quotes, company news and tracking investments.**

Yahoo Finance brings company information and market coverage together, making it useful when you want to look up a business without first building a research dashboard.

**The limit:** Yahoo has both free and paid offerings. Its premium portfolio features include consolidated investment tracking and risk tools, so it would be inaccurate to describe Yahoo as manual tracking with no portfolio analysis. Compare the features you need against [Yahoo's current plans](https://finance.yahoo.com/about/plans/select-plan/) before assuming they are all free.

### 3. Google Finance

**Best fit: market research and a portfolio overview in the Google ecosystem.**

Google Finance is more than a watchlist. Its [June 2026 update](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-finance-updates-june-2026/) introduced expanded portfolio tools, holdings import from files or screenshots, allocation insights and scheduled research updates. It also offers AI research.

**The limit:** Check the experience available to your account and device. Google described a staged mobile rollout; a web feature and a mobile feature are not necessarily available together. If AI-assisted portfolio research is your main need, evaluate Google directly rather than dismissing it as a basic quote page.

### 4. TradingView (Free Tier)

**Best fit: studying price charts and technical indicators.**

TradingView's Basic plan is a practical place to start charting. Its [plan comparison](https://www.tradingview.com/pricing/) lists one chart per tab and two indicators per chart on the free plan.

**The limit:** A large indicator library does not mean you can display every indicator at once. If you need multiple charts or more simultaneous studies, compare the paid allowances. Check the data feed you are using rather than assuming every exchange quote is real-time.

### 5. Finviz (Free Tier)

**Best fit: narrowing a stock universe and getting a visual read on the market.**

[Finviz's screener](https://finviz.com/screener.ashx) and market maps help turn a broad question into a shortlist for further research.

**The limit:** According to its [current data FAQ](https://finviz.com/help/faq), regular US stock quotes are delayed by one minute; Elite provides real-time stock quotes. [Elite](https://finviz.com/elite) also adds advanced screening, exports and other tools. Its free trial converts to a paid subscription unless cancelled, so distinguish an ongoing free account from a temporary Elite trial.

### 6. Koyfin (Free Tier)

**Best fit: company fundamentals and a configurable research workspace.**

Koyfin's [published plan guide](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing-llm-info/) describes a two-year financial snapshot on Free. It also includes a personal portfolio with one account, profit and loss, and exposure information. Koyfin is not simply a data terminal with no portfolio features.

**The limit:** Deeper financial history, broader data access and more advanced portfolio analysis sit in paid plans. Start with one company and check whether the free history answers your question before upgrading.

### 7. OpenBB

**Best fit: developers building a custom research workflow.**

OpenBB's open-source data tooling lets you combine providers and work with their data through a programmable interface. Its [provider documentation](https://docs.openbb.co/odp/python/extensions/providers) distinguishes public data sources from third-party integrations.

**The limit:** You supply the setup and choose the data sources. Many providers require API credentials, and some require paid subscriptions. Open-source software is not a promise of free access to every dataset or every OpenBB product.

## Choose One Workflow Before Adding More Tools

Start with a question about a stock you already follow. Use one research source, check the underlying filing or company announcement, and write down what would change your view. Add charting or screening only when you need it.

For portfolio monitoring, the useful result is a repeatable review: what you believe, what changed, and which evidence deserves your attention. That is the workflow Helm is built around. Try one thesis on Free; consider Pro when you want to follow more of your portfolio in the same way.
