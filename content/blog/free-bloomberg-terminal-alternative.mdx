Bloomberg Terminal combines cross-asset data, news, research, analytics, collaboration and execution workflows. [Ask Bloomberg for a current quote and demonstration](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/bloomberg-terminal/) for the access you need; this guide does not assume a fixed price or contract term.

If you're reading this, you're likely a retail investor, a software engineer with RSUs, a founder managing personal wealth, or a finance professional who doesn't have their firm picking up the tab. You want useful insight into your money at a price you can justify.

Free tools can cover specific research and portfolio-review tasks. Their usefulness depends on the data, history and workflow you need, so compare those limits rather than assuming a percentage of Bloomberg's capabilities.

## What Does Bloomberg Actually Give You?

Before comparing alternatives, it helps to understand what Bloomberg Terminal does and which parts of it actually matter for individual investors. Most people who say they want a "Bloomberg alternative" are really asking for three or four specific capabilities, not the full platform.

### What Matters for Retail Investors

**Market data and quotes.** Check prices, history and company fundamentals, including the coverage and update frequency available on your chosen plan.

**Portfolio analytics.** See your positions, understand allocation and review performance. Check which accounts, assets and calculations a tool supports.

**News and research.** Read reporting, filings and analysis relevant to the companies you follow. Check source access and publication times when timeliness matters.

**Screening.** Find stocks using fundamental or technical criteria. Try your intended filters before paying for additional screening capabilities.

**Charting.** Compare the indicators, chart layouts and price history you use in your own analysis.

### What to Verify for Professional Workflows

**Messaging and collaboration.** If your work depends on Bloomberg's network, a charting or research subscription should not be assumed to provide access to the same participants.

**Fixed income analytics.** Verify coverage for the bonds, curves, credit data and valuation methods your work requires.

**Options data.** Check the instruments covered, quote timing and methodology behind analytics such as implied volatility and Greeks.

**Execution and trading.** Confirm supported brokers, instruments and order workflows. Research access does not establish trading access.

**Licensed data and models.** Verify rights to use and export the data, alongside the valuation or attribution methodology you need.

**Compliance and reporting.** If this is a professional requirement, review the product with your firm's compliance team instead of assuming a consumer subscription meets it.

If a particular fixed income dataset, options workflow or institutional network is essential to your work, verify access directly with its provider. Do not assume a general research subscription supplies it. For equities, ETFs and portfolio review, compare the free options below against the tasks you need to complete.

## The Best Free Bloomberg Terminal Alternatives

### 1. Helm Terminal (Free Tier)

**What it does well:** Portfolio visibility, concentration analysis, and monitoring one investment thesis on Free

[Helm Terminal](/) puts your holdings and investment thesis in one review workflow. Enter positions manually or connect supported accounts through Plaid with read-only access. A brokerage connection is optional.

The free tier brings your holdings into one portfolio view, reports concentration, and includes a general daily market brief. You can save one investment thesis, read up to twelve months of evidence history behind it, and keep it under ongoing monitoring. Checks run on a schedule, so monitoring does not guarantee a new finding or an immediate alert.

Free also shows a harvestable-loss headline figure from the portfolio data available to Helm. The detailed tax lots and wash-sale screening across linked accounts are part of [Helm Pro](/pricing), at $20 a month or $149 a year. Pro also extends monitoring across your tracked theses and adds earnings exposure and a personalized brief.

**Free tier includes:**
- Unlimited account connections (brokerage, bank, retirement)
- Portfolio allocation and concentration analysis
- AI-powered stock analysis for any ticker
- Risk alerts and intelligence feed
- Real-time market data for holdings
- One investment thesis with ongoing monitoring and evidence history
- General daily market brief
- Harvestable-loss headline figure; detailed tax tools require Pro

**Where it falls short vs. Bloomberg:** Helm focuses on equities and ETFs. No fixed income analytics, no options chain analysis, no proprietary news wire. The charting is portfolio-focused, not the deep technical analysis you'd get from a dedicated charting tool.

**Best for:** Investors who want a consolidated portfolio view and evidence to review alongside their reasons for holding a stock.

### 2. Yahoo Finance

**What it does well:** Broad market data, news, basic portfolio tracking

Yahoo Finance is a familiar starting point for quote lookups, news and watchlists, with additional research and analytics on paid plans.

Use it to look up a company's financials, scan headlines and keep a watchlist. Check data coverage and timing for the securities you follow.

**Free tier includes:**
- Stock quotes; coverage and timing depend on the market
- Financial statements, earnings data, analyst estimates
- Manual portfolio tracking and watchlists
- News aggregation
- Screener with basic filters

**Free-plan limits:** Paid tiers add research and portfolio analytics, including performance and risk tools. Check which features are included in your region and plan rather than treating all Yahoo Finance functionality as free. [Yahoo Finance's official plan guide](https://ca.help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN36623.html).

**Best for:** Quick lookups, headline scanning, and basic tracking if you don't mind manual entry.

### 3. Google Finance

**What it does well:** Clean interface, Google ecosystem integration

Google Finance can be a starting point for quote lookups and watchlists. If you use GOOGLEFINANCE in Sheets for custom analysis, check whether the fields, markets and update frequency meet your needs.

The web interface offers quote pages, watchlists and a market overview.

**Free tier includes:**
- Quotes and basic financials; verify timing for your market
- Portfolio watchlists synced to Google account
- GOOGLEFINANCE function in Sheets for custom analysis
- Earnings calendars and market news
- Currency conversion and international market data

**What to check:** Try the data and analysis you need before making it part of your process. A custom spreadsheet requires you to maintain its formulas and verify its inputs.

**Best for:** People who want a clean, simple market overview or are willing to build custom analysis in Google Sheets.

### 4. TradingView (Free Tier)

**What it does well:** Charting, technical analysis, community

TradingView's free Basic plan is a starting point for charting, screening and technical research. It also includes portfolio features; paid tiers expand the limits.

Try its drawing tools, technical indicators, Pine Script and community-published ideas against the charts you use. Compare the free plan's limits with your workflow before upgrading.

**Free tier includes:**
- Advanced charting with 100+ indicators
- Community-published trading ideas
- Basic screener
- Data availability varies by exchange; some real-time feeds require an additional subscription
- One chart per tab (paid unlocks multi-chart layouts)

**Free-plan limits:** Charts, indicators and alerts have usage limits. TradingView also offers fundamentals, portfolios and trading through supported brokers; check the relevant feature and data entitlement in its [official plan comparison](https://www.tradingview.com/pricing/).

**Best for:** Technical traders and chart-first investors who make decisions based on price action.

### 5. Finviz (Free Tier)

**What it does well:** Stock screening, heat maps, visual market overview

Finviz's screener and heat maps give you a compact way to scan stocks and market moves.

The heat maps show market moves across stocks and sectors. Use the screener to try your fundamental and technical filters before deciding whether you need paid features.

**Free tier includes:**
- Stock screener with 60+ filters
- Market heat maps (S&P 500, sectors, world)
- Basic charting and technical analysis
- Fundamental data tables
- Insider trading data

**Free-plan limits:** Free stock quotes are delayed. Finviz has portfolio features, while Elite adds real-time stock data, expanded tools, alerts and exports. Check its [current Free and Elite comparison](https://finviz.com/elite) for the limits that affect your workflow.

**Best for:** Stock screening, market visualization, and idea generation.

### 6. Koyfin (Free Tier)

**What it does well:** Financial data depth, professional-grade research

Koyfin combines company fundamentals, market dashboards, charting and portfolio tools in a research workspace.

Use its financial statements, valuation metrics and estimates to investigate a company or compare peers, within the Free plan's history and usage limits.

**Free tier includes:**
- Financial statements and fundamentals for global stocks
- Valuation and growth metrics
- Basic charting with fundamental overlays
- Economic data and indicators
- Watchlists and basic screening

**Free-plan limits:** Koyfin currently lists two years of financials, one year of estimates and My Portfolios on Free. Deeper history, advanced portfolio analytics and advisor integrations depend on the plan. [Koyfin's current pricing and features](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing/).

**Best for:** Company fundamentals and peer comparisons in a market-data workspace.

### 7. OpenBB

**What it does well:** Open-source, developer-friendly, extensible

OpenBB has two different starting points: a free hosted Community edition and the open-source Open Data Platform for configurable data workflows. Python setup is not a requirement for all OpenBB access.

For technical users, the Open Data Platform connects public, licensed and proprietary data through interfaces including Python and a command line. [Open Data Platform documentation](https://docs.openbb.co/odp).

**Free starting points:**
- Hosted Community edition for individual use
- Separate open-source Open Data Platform for configurable research
- Data access that depends on the sources and credentials you connect

**Free-plan limits:** Included data access is limited, and connected providers may charge separately. Commercial editions have separate deployment and team features. [OpenBB's current editions](https://openbb.co/pricing/).

**Best for:** Investors building a configurable research workspace, with an additional open-source route for developers.

## A Practical Weekly Portfolio Review

Here are four useful tasks to check against your own process:

1. **Know what you own.** Total allocation across all accounts. Which positions are your largest. How concentrated you are in any single stock or sector.
2. **Know what changed.** Which holdings moved significantly. Whether any earnings reports affected your positions. If your allocation drifted from where you want it.
3. **Know what to review.** Unrealized losses, allocation drift, and concentration risk. Detailed tax-lot analysis and wash-sale screening need to be distinguished from a headline loss figure.
4. **Research before buying.** Basic fundamentals, analyst sentiment, recent news for stocks you're considering.

Keep this weekly review separate from professional requirements such as licensed data access, specialized analytics or trading integrations. Those requirements need their own evaluation.

Helm's free tier covers portfolio visibility, concentration, a general brief, and a headline harvestable-loss figure. Detailed tax analysis and earnings exposure require Pro. For item 4, consider Koyfin or Yahoo Finance for company research, and TradingView for charting and technical analysis.

A realistic free stack for most retail investors looks like:

- **Helm Terminal Free** for portfolio visibility, concentration, one monitored thesis, a general brief, and a harvestable-loss headline figure
- **Koyfin free** for deep fundamental research
- **TradingView free** for charting (if you use technical analysis)
- **Yahoo Finance** for quick lookups and news

Total cost for those free tiers: $0. Helm's detailed tax tools, broader thesis monitoring, earnings exposure, and personalized brief require Pro at $20 a month or $149 a year.

## Check These Limits Before Choosing a Free Plan

The important question is whether a particular plan supplies what your process requires. Check:

**Options coverage.** Confirm the exchanges, contracts, quote timing and analytics included.

**Fixed income coverage.** Check the securities and pricing sources you need, rather than assuming stock-research access includes them.

**Collaboration access.** Identify the people and systems you need to work with and confirm that the product supports that workflow.

**Historical data.** Check the available period, frequency, adjustments and export limits for each dataset. There is no single history limit that describes every free plan.

**Update frequency.** Confirm whether a feed is real-time, delayed or end-of-day and whether a separate exchange subscription is required.

If a particular dataset or professional workflow is essential, verify it directly with the provider.
