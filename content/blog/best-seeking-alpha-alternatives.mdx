## What are you replacing in Seeking Alpha?

Seeking Alpha combines contributor analysis, quantitative ratings, screeners and research tools. Its [official Premium page](https://help.seekingalpha.com/what-is-seeking-alpha-premium) lists **$299 per year plus applicable tax or VAT**. Check your actual renewal or checkout price, especially if you joined on an introductory offer.

Its contributor model is useful when you want to compare arguments about the same company. The tradeoff is the work of judging each author's assumptions, evidence and time horizon. More opinions do not automatically resolve a decision.

Two distinctions matter when comparing alternatives:

- **Quant Ratings include forecasts.** Seeking Alpha uses financial statements, price performance and analyst estimates, including revisions. Its five factor grades feed a broader rating; the overall rating is not simply a letter grade or an average of the factors. Treat it as an input to research, with assumptions to examine. See [Seeking Alpha's methodology FAQ](https://help.seekingalpha.com/premium/quant-ratings-and-factor-grades-faq).
- **Seeking Alpha does track positions.** Premium and Pro support [brokerage linking, holdings and cost basis](https://help.seekingalpha.com/premium/how-can-i-link-my-seeking-alpha-portfolio-with-my-broker), and the portfolio supports [manual share and purchase-price entry](https://help.seekingalpha.com/how-can-i-input-the-amount-of-shares-i-have-bought-and-the-share-price-into-my-portfolio). Confirm that your broker is supported. Portfolio connection alone is not a reason to choose Helm over it.

## 1. Stock Analysis, if you mainly wanted the data

Stock Analysis is a practical starting point for company financials and screening. Try its free access on the tickers you follow before paying for more history or exports.

Its [official Pro page](https://stockanalysis.com/pro/) lists **$9.99 per month or $79 per year**. Pro includes access to data on more than 130,000 global stocks and funds, longer financial history, brokerage syncing and one download per day. Unlimited adds higher usage limits, including unlimited downloads, and is listed at $16.58 per month when billed annually.

**Good at:** comparing company numbers with a low-cost paid option.

**Tradeoff:** a financial table still needs interpretation. If the main value you get from Seeking Alpha is a detailed contributor argument, test whether the data workflow answers the same questions for you.

## 2. TIKR, if you wanted financial history and estimates

[TIKR's Free plan](https://www.tikr.com/pricing) covers US stocks with five years and eight quarters of financials, 90 days of transcripts and one year of forecast analyst estimates. Plus opens global coverage, ten years of financial history and two years of forecast estimates at **$24.95 per month**. Pro and Ultimate offer further data and tools.

Twelve monthly Plus payments total $299.40. That is close to Seeking Alpha Premium's standard annual price, but buys a different research workflow. TIKR also offers annual billing; compare the current annual checkout price separately.

**Good at:** working through financial history, estimates and transcripts yourself.

**Tradeoff:** you need to decide which assumptions matter and build your own argument from the data. More history does not settle the investment case.

## 3. Morningstar Investor, if you wanted an analyst valuation framework

[Morningstar Investor](https://www.morningstar.com/mm/investor/stock-analysis) centers on its analysts' fair value estimates, stock ratings and economic moat assessments. Its portfolio tools include X-Ray and Stock Intersection for examining allocation, fees and overlapping holdings.

The official product page lists **$34.95 per month or $249 per year**, with a seven-day trial for eligible users.

**Good at:** applying a defined valuation framework to company and portfolio research.

**Tradeoff:** fair value estimates depend on assumptions about the business and its future. Check coverage of your own holdings and read the reasoning behind a rating before subscribing.

For a closer comparison, see [Best Morningstar Alternatives](/blog/best-morningstar-alternatives).

## 4. Koyfin, if you wanted a research dashboard

[Koyfin](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing/) brings charts, market and macro dashboards, financials and portfolio tools into a configurable workspace. Free includes two years of financials, one year of estimates, two watchlists, two screens and two custom dashboards.

Plus is **$39 per month** and adds longer financial and estimate history, unlimited custom dashboards, filings and transcripts. Premium is **$79 per month** and adds deeper portfolio analytics and customization. Annual billing discounts are available on its pricing page.

**Good at:** putting company research in wider market context and organizing a repeatable dashboard.

**Tradeoff:** choosing the charts and comparisons takes setup. Use the free dashboards first to see whether that workflow is valuable enough to maintain.

## 5. Simply Wall St, if you wanted visual company research

Simply Wall St's [Snowflake framework](https://support.simplywall.st/hc/en-us/articles/9423775242383-Understanding-the-Portfolio-Returns-Analysis-Calculations) summarizes valuation, future performance, past performance, financial health and dividends. That gives you a consistent set of questions to explore across companies.

Its [plan comparison](https://simplywall.st/plans) lists five company reports per month on Free, with one portfolio and ten holdings. Premium increases those to 30 reports a month and three portfolios with 30 holdings each; Unlimited lifts those limits. Use the provider's checkout for current paid prices.

**Good at:** a visual overview that helps organize an initial company review.

**Tradeoff:** a summary is a starting point. Read the analysis behind it and check the valuation assumptions against the company documents before relying on the shape alone.

See [Best Simply Wall St Alternatives](/blog/best-simply-wall-st-alternatives) for more options.

## 6. SEC EDGAR and company investor relations, for original sources

[SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) gives you free access to company filings, including annual and quarterly reports. Start with the business description, risk factors, management discussion and financial statements. Check the company's investor relations site for earnings releases, presentations and any available call recordings or transcripts.

**Good at:** checking what the company actually disclosed and when.

**Tradeoff:** you have to find, read and compare the documents yourself. Company disclosures also contain management's judgments; being a primary source does not make every forecast certain.

## How to choose without buying another unused subscription

Pick one stock you already understand and ask the same question in two tools: What assumptions support the valuation, what could invalidate them, and where is the evidence?

For numbers, start with Stock Analysis or TIKR Free. For an analyst valuation framework, look at Morningstar. For dashboards, try Koyfin Free; for a visual overview, try Simply Wall St's free reports. Keep EDGAR available to check the underlying disclosures.
