A thesis tracker is useful if it helps you answer a specific question: **has the evidence changed the reason I own this stock?** A dashboard full of prices cannot answer that on its own. Neither can an alert that does not explain which reason it tests.

I build Helm. This is a guide to choosing a workflow, with links to providers, rather than an independent ranking of products I have paid to test. Public offers were checked September 19, 2026; availability and pricing can change.

## Start with the job

| Your problem | What to look for | What to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I forget why I bought | A journal or worksheet | Can I record evidence, a review date and what would change my mind? |
| I need to develop an idea | A research tool | Can I open the source behind the argument? |
| I do not revisit my reasons | A thesis monitor | Does a new finding test my saved reason, or just mention the ticker? |
| I track several independent ideas | Broader monitoring | Can I keep each thesis under review at a price that makes sense? |

These categories overlap. Begin with one stock and a reason you can test, rather than importing your entire portfolio and hoping the tool infers your convictions.

## Run a one-thesis test

1. **Write one reason.** For example, a hypothetical margin thesis might require operating margin to remain above a threshold you choose. This is an example of a testable reason, not a recommendation for any company.
2. **Keep the starting evidence.** Record the document, reporting period and relevant passage. A copied number without a period is easy to misread later.
3. **Define what would change your mind.** Use an observable condition. Decide whether one weak quarter warrants a review or whether your reason explicitly depends on a longer trend.
4. **Inspect the tool's finding.** Does the linked document say what the finding claims? Does it test that reason? A quotation can be accurate while the interpretation is wrong.
5. **Return after new evidence arrives.** A setup completed once does not establish that ongoing monitoring is useful.

Use the [printable thesis review worksheet](/resources/helm-thesis-review-worksheet.pdf) without an account, or read the [worked thesis examples](/blog/investment-thesis-examples). There is no need to subscribe to discover whether you can write a reason worth monitoring.

## When paying makes sense

Paying for broader monitoring makes sense when you have additional theses you want to revisit and the first one has already produced useful evidence or a clearer review process. More imported holdings alone are not a reason to upgrade.
