# RSU Tax Strategies: A Complete Guide for Tech Employees

RSU tax planning is one of the most consequential financial decisions tech employees face — yet most people handle it on autopilot. Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) are taxed as ordinary income when they vest, which means a single vesting event can push you into a higher tax bracket, trigger AMT implications, and create concentration risk you never intended to carry.

This guide explains exactly how RSUs are taxed, the most effective RSU tax strategies, and how to build a systematic approach to managing equity compensation across your career.

## How Are RSUs Taxed? The Two Tax Events

RSUs create two distinct taxable events. Understanding both is critical to effective tax planning.

### Tax Event 1: Vesting (Ordinary Income)

When RSUs vest, the fair market value (FMV) of the shares on the vesting date is treated as ordinary income — exactly like salary. Your employer reports this on your W-2 and typically withholds taxes by selling a portion of the vesting shares ("sell to cover").

**Example:**
- 500 shares vest when stock price is $150/share
- Taxable income: 500 x $150 = $75,000
- Added to your W-2 as compensation income
- Federal + state + FICA taxes apply at your marginal rate

**Standard withholding rates:**
| Tax Type | Withholding Rate | Actual Rate (may differ) |
|----------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Federal | 22% (supplemental rate) | Up to 37% depending on bracket |
| State | Varies | CA: up to 13.3%, WA/TX: 0% |
| Social Security | 6.2% (up to wage base) | 6.2% |
| Medicare | 1.45% (+0.9% over $200K) | 1.45-2.35% |

**Critical insight**: The 22% federal supplemental withholding is almost always insufficient for high earners. If your total comp exceeds $200K, you likely owe 32-37% federal. This under-withholding means a surprise tax bill in April unless you plan for it.

### Tax Event 2: Sale (Capital Gains/Losses)

When you eventually sell RSU shares, you pay capital gains tax on the difference between sale price and your cost basis. Your cost basis is the FMV at vesting — the price at which you already paid income tax.

- **Held less than 1 year after vesting**: Short-term capital gains (taxed as ordinary income)
- **Held more than 1 year after vesting**: Long-term capital gains (15% or 20% rate)

**Example (continuing from above):**
- Cost basis: $150/share (the vesting date FMV)
- You sell 6 months later at $180/share
- Capital gain: $30/share x 500 shares = $15,000 (short-term)
- Or you sell 14 months later at $180/share
- Same $15,000 gain, but taxed at 15% (long-term) instead of 32%+ (ordinary income)

If the stock drops below your vesting price, you have a [capital loss](/blog/capital-gains-vs-losses-explained) — which can offset other gains or up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year.

## The 7 Most Effective RSU Tax Strategies

### Strategy 1: Immediate Diversification (Sell at Vest)

**The strategy**: Sell RSU shares immediately when they vest. Reinvest proceeds into a [diversified portfolio](/blog/portfolio-diversification-guide).

**Why it works**: You eliminate concentration risk and make an active investment decision rather than passively accumulating single-stock exposure. The tax treatment is identical whether you sell at vest or hold for 10 seconds — both result in ordinary income tax on the vesting value with negligible capital gain.

**Best for**: Most people. Studies consistently show that diversified portfolios outperform single-stock holdings on a risk-adjusted basis. If you would not buy $75,000 of your company's stock today with cash, you should not hold $75,000 of it just because it vested as RSUs.

### Strategy 2: Hold for Long-Term Capital Gains

**The strategy**: Hold RSU shares for at least 12 months after vesting to qualify for long-term capital gains rates on any appreciation.

**Tax savings**: If shares appreciate 20% and your marginal rate is 35%, holding saves you approximately 15-20 percentage points on the gain portion.

**The risk**: You are making a concentrated bet that your company stock will appreciate enough to justify both the tax benefit AND the diversification cost. If the stock drops 20% while you wait for long-term treatment, the tax savings are irrelevant.

**Best for**: Employees with high conviction in near-term stock appreciation AND whose RSU position is small relative to total net worth (<10% of portfolio).

### Strategy 3: Estimated Tax Payments

**The strategy**: Make quarterly estimated tax payments to cover the gap between 22% supplemental withholding and your actual marginal rate.

**How to calculate:**
1. Estimate total annual income (salary + RSU vestings + bonus)
2. Determine your effective federal rate (likely 32-37% for total comp over $200K)
3. Calculate the withholding gap: (actual rate - 22%) x RSU income
4. Pay this gap via quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES)

**Best for**: Everyone with RSU income. Avoiding underpayment penalties is not optional — it is table stakes.

### Strategy 4: Maximize Tax-Advantaged Accounts

**The strategy**: Use RSU proceeds to maximize contributions to all available tax-advantaged accounts.

**2026 contribution limits:**
| Account | Limit | Tax Benefit |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| 401(k) | $23,500 | Pre-tax (reduces current income) |
| Backdoor Roth IRA | $7,000 | Tax-free growth |
| HSA (family) | $8,550 | Triple tax advantage |
| Mega Backdoor Roth | Up to $70,000 total | Tax-free growth |
| 529 Plan | Varies by state | State deduction + tax-free growth |

If your employer offers a Mega Backdoor Roth (after-tax 401k contributions with in-plan Roth conversion), this is one of the most powerful tools available to high-earning tech employees.

### Strategy 5: Tax-Loss Harvesting with RSU Shares

**The strategy**: If RSU shares have dropped below their vesting price, sell to realize the capital loss. Use losses to offset capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio or deduct up to $3,000 against ordinary income.

**Important**: The wash sale rule prevents you from repurchasing substantially identical securities within 30 days. You cannot sell company stock at a loss and immediately rebuy it. You can, however, buy a sector ETF that includes your company as one of many holdings.

**Best for**: Employees whose company stock has declined after vesting. This strategy converts a bad outcome (stock dropped) into a tax benefit.

### Strategy 6: Charitable Giving with Appreciated RSU Shares

**The strategy**: Donate appreciated RSU shares (held >1 year) directly to charity or a Donor-Advised Fund (DAF). You get a charitable deduction for the full market value without paying capital gains tax on the appreciation.

**Example:**
- RSU shares vested at $100 (cost basis), now worth $200
- Donate $50,000 worth directly to DAF
- Deduction: $50,000 (reduces taxable income)
- Capital gains tax avoided: $50,000 gain x 23.8% = $11,900

**Best for**: Employees who plan to donate to charity anyway and hold appreciated RSU shares longer than 1 year.

### Strategy 7: Net Exercise Planning for Stock Options

While this guide focuses on RSUs, many tech employees also hold ISOs or NSOs. If you have both, coordinate exercise timing with RSU vesting dates to manage your total taxable income across the year and avoid bracket creep.

## Building a Systematic RSU Management Process

### Quarterly RSU Review Checklist

1. **Concentration check**: What percentage of your total portfolio is company stock? (Target: under 10-15%)
2. **Tax withholding review**: Is year-to-date withholding tracking toward your actual liability?
3. **Upcoming vests**: What vests next quarter? Plan diversification in advance
4. **Loss harvesting scan**: Are any vested lots underwater? Consider harvesting
5. **Contribution room**: Have you maximized all tax-advantaged accounts?

### Tracking RSU Tax Lots

Every vesting event creates a new tax lot with a unique cost basis. Maintaining lot-level records is essential for:

- Identifying specific lots to sell (highest cost basis first to minimize gains)
- Tracking holding periods for long-term vs. short-term treatment
- Accurate capital gains reporting on your tax return

## State Tax Considerations

RSU taxation varies dramatically by state. This matters especially for employees who relocate.

| State | Income Tax on RSU Vesting | Notes |
|-------|---------------------------|-------|
| California | Up to 13.3% | Taxes RSUs allocated to CA work days |
| New York | Up to 10.9% (+NYC 3.88%) | NY sources rule applies |
| Washington | 0% | Plus no capital gains tax |
| Texas | 0% | Major advantage for RSU recipients |
| Colorado | 4.4% flat | Relatively favorable |

**Multi-state allocation**: If you were granted RSUs in California but moved to Washington before vesting, California may still tax a portion based on the ratio of CA work days between grant and vest. Consult a CPA familiar with multi-state equity compensation.

## When to Consult a Tax Professional

DIY RSU tax management works for straightforward situations — a single employer, one state, standard vesting schedule. Seek professional guidance if:

- Total equity compensation exceeds $500K annually
- You are exercising ISOs alongside RSU vesting (AMT implications)
- You have moved states between grant and vesting dates
- You are considering an 83(b) election on early-exercised options
- You are approaching the $1M+ income threshold (additional Medicare tax, Pease limitation, NIIT)

## The Bottom Line

RSUs are not free money — they are ordinary income delivered in stock form. The default approach (sell to cover, hold the rest, ignore until tax time) leaves thousands of dollars on the table annually. Build a systematic process: diversify at vest by default, manage tax brackets proactively, maximize tax-advantaged space, and treat your equity compensation as the significant financial event it is.
