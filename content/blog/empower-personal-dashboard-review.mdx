Empower Personal Dashboard is the free net worth and retirement planning product formerly known as Personal Capital, and it is worth separating two things that get talked about as one: the free dashboard, which is genuinely free and genuinely useful for seeing every account in one place, and the advisory business behind it, which manages portfolios for a fee once a balance clears six figures. Most reviews blur the two. This one keeps them apart. Everything below was checked against empower.com in September 2026.

## What the free dashboard actually does

The core of Empower Personal Dashboard is account aggregation: link checking, savings, credit cards, mortgages, loans, 401(k)s, IRAs and brokerage accounts, and the dashboard rolls them into one net worth number that updates as balances change. Empower's own net worth page describes it as a way to "see all of your accounts in one secure place" and "track your complete, true net worth" ([empower.com/net-worth](https://www.empower.com/net-worth)).

The aggregation itself runs through a third party. Empower's terms of use state directly: "For aggregation services, Empower utilizes Yodlee, Inc's Fastlink platform. Yodlee, Inc. is an Envestnet company and is subject to bank-level data security and examination by the U.S. Federal Banking Agencies" ([empower.com/hpc/terms-of-use](https://www.empower.com/hpc/terms-of-use)). Practically, that means account credentials are held by Yodlee rather than stored directly by Empower.

Past account linking, the free dashboard is built around three named tools:

**Retirement Planner** models retirement scenarios against linked accounts rather than a static calculator. Empower describes it as letting a user "test-drive what retirement could look like" and "test different retirement ages and scenarios" ([empower.com/tools/retirement-planner](https://www.empower.com/tools/retirement-planner)).

**Investment Checkup** (listed on the main tools page under the heading Portfolio Analysis) scores the linked portfolio against a target allocation. Empower's own page describes the tool as a way to "assess your portfolio risk," "analyze past performance" and "get an alternative allocation" ([empower.com/investment-checkup](https://www.empower.com/investment-checkup)).

**Fee Analyzer** estimates what a linked portfolio pays in fund expenses. Empower does not currently keep a dedicated marketing page live for it, so the clearest description comes from a third-party walkthrough: it "calculates a weighted average expense ratio for all of your linked accounts" and projects what that cost compounds to over time, with one worked example showing a 0.06% weighted expense ratio costing "$17,865 over the next seven years" ([robberger.com/empower-review](https://robberger.com/empower-review/)). The tool's own support documentation is titled "Retirement Fee Analyzer Calculations Overview," confirming it still exists inside the product even without a current standalone landing page.

Rounding out the free tier, Empower's tools page also lists Budgeting & Cash Flow ("daily monitoring and expense tracking"), Savings Planner, Debt Paydown, Emergency Fund planning, a Transactions view, and a tax filing referral partnership rather than a built-in filer ([empower.com/tools](https://www.empower.com/tools)). None of it requires a paid plan.

## The advisory upsell, and what it costs

The free dashboard is also how Empower finds clients for its paid side, Empower Advisory Group, which runs under three named services and one flat family tier.

**Personal Strategy** opens at a $100,000 minimum and includes "dedicated" advisor access, tax optimization and disciplined rebalancing according to Empower's own product page ([empower.com/products-solutions/personal-strategy](https://www.empower.com/products-solutions/personal-strategy)).

**Personal Strategy Wealth Management** opens at $250,000 and adds "two dedicated financial advisors" and specialists in areas like real estate and stock options, with individual stock positions used for tax optimization rather than only funds ([empower.com/products-solutions/wealth-management](https://www.empower.com/products-solutions/wealth-management)).

**Empower Private Client** opens at a $1,000,000 minimum and adds financial planning specialists across tax, retirement, estate, real estate and charitable planning, plus daily portfolio monitoring and tax-loss harvesting ([empower.com/products-solutions/private-client](https://www.empower.com/products-solutions/private-client)).

The fee schedule, per Empower's Personal Strategy page, charges 0.89% annually on assets from $100,000 to $999,999, covering both the Personal Strategy and Wealth Management minimums. Above $1,000,000, Private Client's page lists a tiered rate: 0.79% on the first $3,000,000, 0.69% on the next $2,000,000 ($3,000,001 to $5,000,000), 0.59% on the next $5,000,000 ($5,000,001 to $10,000,000), and 0.49% on any amount over $10,000,000. A separate family tier lets parents and children of a Private Client pay a flat 0.79% with no minimum. Every one of those figures comes from the two product pages above; a third-party review of the same schedule lands on the identical breakpoints ([nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/empower](https://www.nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/empower)). Empower's pages describe the fee as "all-in" alongside the underlying fund expense ratios, with no separate trading commissions.

## The advisory upsell

The free dashboard is the top of a funnel Empower publishes plainly: the Personal Strategy, Wealth Management and Private Client pages above are the paid tiers, and the $100,000 minimum is the line where a free user becomes a prospect. Empower's own pages do not describe how or when free users are contacted, so this post does not claim a pattern. The published facts are the minimums and the fee schedule, and those are the ones that decide whether the free tools come with a sales conversation attached.

## What the free dashboard does not do

Set against what Empower actually publishes, a few things are consistently absent from the free tier. Tax-loss harvesting and wash-sale handling appear only on the Private Client page, gated behind a $1,000,000 minimum; the free tools, Retirement Planner, Investment Checkup and Fee Analyzer, never mention tax lots or loss harvesting in their own descriptions. None of Empower's tool pages describe tracking earnings dates against individual holdings. None describe reading a 10-K, 10-Q or 8-K. The Investment Checkup evaluates allocation and risk in aggregate; it is not built to explain why a specific position moved or what a specific company just filed. Empower is also a US-focused personal finance product; its pages describe domestic account types (401(k), IRA, brokerage, mortgage) and do not describe holding or analyzing foreign-listed securities.

## Who it fits

The free dashboard fits almost anyone who wants one net worth number pulled from scattered accounts and does not already have a spreadsheet they trust. The Retirement Planner and Investment Checkup are strong enough on their own to justify linking accounts even for someone with no interest in the paid side. The fit gets narrower once a balance nears six figures: at that point the free tools and the advisory sales funnel start to overlap, and the choice becomes whether a 0.89% annual fee for a dedicated advisor is worth it against managing the same accounts unassisted, using the same free dashboard for tracking.

## Where Helm fits

I build Helm Terminal, so weigh this section accordingly. Helm is not a net worth dashboard and does not compete with what Empower's free tools do well. It reads brokerage accounts read-only, then does the position-level work Empower's tools do not: concentration and earnings exposure across accounts, tax-loss candidates with wash-sale screening, and a written thesis monitored against filings and market reporting for the positions someone actually holds. Empower answers "what is my whole financial picture worth." Helm answers "what is happening to the things I own, and does it still match why I bought them." The two describe different jobs, not competing versions of the same one.

## Verdict

The free Empower Personal Dashboard earns its reputation: account aggregation through Yodlee, a genuinely useful Retirement Planner, an Investment Checkup that will flag concentration most people have not noticed, and a Fee Analyzer that puts a real number on what fund expenses cost over time, all at no charge. The tradeoff is that the free product is also a lead funnel, and reviews outside Empower describe real outreach once a linked balance looks advisory-worthy. The advisory tiers themselves are priced in line with the industry, from 0.89% down to 0.49% as assets scale, but they start at a $100,000 minimum and the free tools already cover most of what a self-directed person needs day to day. For anyone who wants the dashboard without the advisory conversation, that is a known tradeoff rather than a surprise, once the numbers above are in view.
