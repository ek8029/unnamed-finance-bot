# Best Empower (Personal Capital) Alternatives in 2026

Empower (formerly Personal Capital) built its reputation as the best free portfolio tracker and net worth dashboard. For years, it was the go-to recommendation for anyone wanting to see all their financial accounts in one place. But since the rebrand and increased focus on selling advisory services, many users are searching for **Personal Capital alternatives** that maintain the tracking magic without the constant upsells.

Whether you are frustrated by Empower's advisory sales calls, concerned about the platform's direction post-rebrand, or simply want a tool with better intelligence features, this guide covers the best alternatives in 2026.

## Why Leave Empower (Personal Capital)?

The platform still works, but several trends are pushing users toward alternatives:

- **Advisory upselling**: If your accounts cross $100K+, expect persistent calls from their advisory team
- **Feature stagnation**: The free dashboard has seen minimal innovation since the Empower acquisition
- **Rebrand confusion**: The shift from Personal Capital to Empower fractured the user experience and trust
- **Basic intelligence**: It tracks balances well but provides minimal actionable insight about what to DO with your money
- **Slow account syncing**: Many users report increasing reliability issues with account connections
- **Mint refugees**: With Mint shutting down in 2024, many former Mint users tried Empower but found it lacking for their needs

If you want a wealth tracking tool that is actually innovating in 2026, these alternatives deliver.

If you are a former Mint user who tried Empower and found it too investment-focused (or too pushy with advisory sales), several alternatives below cater specifically to your needs. Monarch Money and Copilot have absorbed the largest share of Mint refugees.

## 2. Monarch Money

**Best for: Former Mint users who want budgeting AND net worth tracking**

Monarch Money has positioned itself as the premium Mint replacement. It combines budgeting, net worth tracking, and basic investment visibility in a polished modern interface.

### Key Features

- Full budgeting system with categories, goals, and recurring detection
- Net worth tracking across all account types (bank, investment, property, crypto)
- Collaboration features for couples (shared finances)
- Cash flow forecasting and bill tracking
- Custom reports and flexible date ranges
- Clean, modern UI that feels like a 2026 product

### Limitations

- No free tier (just a 7-day trial)
- Investment analysis is surface-level — no automated intelligence
- No proactive alerts about portfolio risk or concentration
- Primarily a budgeting tool that also shows investments, not an investment tool
- No real-time market data

### Pricing

$10/month or $90/year after 7-day free trial.

### Verdict

Monarch is the best Empower alternative if budgeting is your primary need and investment tracking is secondary. It is what Mint should have become.

## 3. Copilot

**Best for: Apple users who want beautiful design + personal finance tracking**

Copilot is an iOS and Mac-first finance app that prioritizes design and user experience above all else. It has won Apple Design Awards and attracted users who care as much about aesthetics as functionality.

### Key Features

- Stunning iOS and Mac native design
- Automatic transaction categorization with AI
- Net worth tracking across linked accounts
- Subscription tracking and bill monitoring
- Investment performance with simple visualizations
- Yearly and monthly trend analysis

### Limitations

- Apple ecosystem only (no Android, no web app)
- No free tier — starts at $11/month
- Investment features are basic (portfolio display, not intelligence)
- No proactive portfolio alerts or risk analysis
- Limited customization compared to Empower's dashboards
- No retirement planning tools

### Pricing

$11/month or $70/year (Apple only).

### Verdict

Copilot is the premium choice for Apple users who want a beautiful daily finance experience. It does not match Empower's investment depth, but it far exceeds it in design quality.

## 4. Kubera

**Best for: High-net-worth individuals tracking alternative assets**

Kubera is built for wealthy investors whose portfolios extend beyond stocks and bonds into real estate, crypto, private equity, collectibles, and international holdings. If Empower cannot handle your asset complexity, Kubera can.

### Key Features

- Track any asset class: stocks, crypto, real estate, art, private equity, domains, vehicles
- Crypto wallet and exchange connections (DeFi included)
- International bank and brokerage support
- Beneficiary and estate planning documents
- Net worth tracking with custom manual assets
- Multi-currency support

### Limitations

- No free tier ($15/month minimum)
- No automated investment intelligence or proactive alerts
- Interface is functional but not particularly inspiring
- No budgeting or cash flow features
- Limited analysis — primarily a tracking and display tool
- Smaller company with less development velocity

### Pricing

$15/month (individual), $22/month (family).

### Verdict

Kubera is the right choice if your wealth is distributed across alternative and international assets that Empower and traditional tools cannot connect. For standard US brokerage accounts, other options offer more intelligence.

## 5. New Retirement (now Boldin)

**Best for: Pre-retirees and retirees focused on financial planning**

New Retirement (recently rebranded to Boldin) is the most comprehensive retirement planning tool available to individual investors. While Empower offers basic retirement projections, New Retirement builds detailed plans accounting for Social Security, pensions, tax optimization, and withdrawal strategies.

### Key Features

- Detailed retirement planning with Monte Carlo simulations
- Social Security optimization strategies
- Tax-efficient withdrawal planning (Roth conversions, RMDs)
- Estate planning projections
- Account linking for current balance tracking
- "What if" scenario modeling (early retirement, market crashes, health costs)

### Limitations

- Focused almost exclusively on retirement planning — not general portfolio intelligence
- No proactive daily monitoring of holdings
- Limited value for investors under 40
- No budgeting or spending tracking
- Real-time market data is not a priority
- Interface is functional but information-dense

### Pricing

Free (basic plan), Plus at $10/month with full planning features.

### Verdict

If you are within 15 years of retirement and your primary concern is "will my money last?", New Retirement/Boldin answers that question better than any alternative. For investors focused on growing and monitoring their portfolio today, it is less relevant.
