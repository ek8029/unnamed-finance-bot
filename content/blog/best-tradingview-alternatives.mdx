If TradingView is not covering what you need, the alternatives split into three groups: dedicated charting platforms (StockCharts, TrendSpider), platforms bundled free with a brokerage account (Webull Desktop, TradeStation Desktop, MetaTrader 5), and research tools that were never about charts at all (Finviz, Koyfin). Which group fits depends on whether the gap is price, automation, execution, or what happens after a position is already in your account.

TradingView's charting is the best available at its price, the free plan is usable, and Pine Script plus the community library means someone has already built almost any indicator you can name. This is not a post about leaving it. It is a post about the specific things it does not do, because those are the reasons people search for alternatives in the first place.

Prices below were checked in September 2026 on each vendor's own page. Where I could not verify a figure from the vendor, I say so instead of guessing.

## What TradingView costs, as the baseline

The [TradingView pricing page](https://www.tradingview.com/pricing/) lists five plans. Basic is free with no card required. It gives 2 indicators per chart, 1 chart per tab, 3 price alerts, and ads.

The paid tiers, billed annually:

- **Essential**: $12.95 a month, $155.40 a year. 5 indicators per chart, 2 charts per tab, 20 price alerts and 20 technical alerts, no ads.
- **Plus**: $29.95 a month, $359.40 a year. 10 indicators, 4 charts, 100 of each alert type.
- **Premium**: $59.95 a month, $719.40 a year. 25 indicators, 8 charts, 400 of each alert type.
- **Ultimate**: $199.95 a month, $2,399.40 a year. 50 indicators, 16 charts, 1,000 of each alert type.

The page states that annual billing saves $24, $60, $120 and $480 a year on those four tiers against month-to-month billing, which it did not display in the view I could load. Essential, Plus and Premium come with 30-day trials; Ultimate has 14 days.

What TradingView does not do, in the order people usually notice: it does not execute trades on its own (it routes to partner brokers), it does not automate the analysis for you, it does not know what you own across accounts, and the fundamentals and screening are secondary to the chart. Each tool below covers one of those.

## 1. TrendSpider, if the gap is automation

[TrendSpider](https://trendspider.com/pricing/) is charting with the technical analysis done by software instead of by hand. Automated trendline and pattern detection, multi-timeframe scanning, strategy backtesting, trading bots, and alerts that fire on conditions rather than on a price level. The pricing page describes every plan as including charting, technical analysis, scanning, strategy development and an AI assistant.

Pricing is the messiest of any tool here. Billed monthly, Standard lists at $82, Premium at $137, Enhanced at $183, with an Advanced tier above. The page shows first-year promotional prices with the list price crossed out, and those promotions rotate, so the annual figure you see will depend on the day. The 14-day trial is paid, not free: $19 for Standard, $29 for Premium.

The plan differences are about capacity. Standard has 5 open workspaces, 5 trading bots, 10 active alerts and backtesting at 2-hour resolution. Premium lifts that to 10 workspaces, 10 bots, 50 alerts and 5-minute backtesting.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** draws the trendlines, runs the scans and backtests the rules without you doing it manually.
**What it lacks:** fundamentals of any depth, a community script library, and a price that makes sense for anyone who is not trading patterns systematically.

## 2. StockCharts, if the gap is scanning and price

[StockCharts](https://stockcharts.com/pricing/) predates TradingView and still runs the most complete scan engine in retail charting. ChartLists, saved scans, scheduled scans, and price datasets going back to 1980 (1900 on the higher tiers) are the core.

The free plan gives 3 indicators or overlays per chart, 1 ChartList, 1 screener, 1 scan and 2 price alerts. Paid tiers:

- **Basic**: $19.95 a month, or $239.40 a year for 12 months plus 1 free. 25 indicators, 250 ChartLists, 200 scans, 100 price alerts.
- **Extra**: $29.95 a month, or $359.40 a year. 500 ChartLists, 500 scans, 500 price alerts, 10 price datasets per chart, 5-second refresh, history from 1900.
- **PRO**: $49.95 a month, or $599.40 a year. Adds 50 saved layouts, 250 advanced alerts and 25 scheduled scans.

The first month is a free trial at the Extra level. Real-time US data is a separate $9.95 a month add-on; the free data plan uses BATS real-time for US and delayed international.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** scheduled scans that run on their own and land in a ChartList, and a longer historical record.
**What it lacks:** the drawing tools and chart polish. The interface has not changed much in years, and there is no scripting language on the level of Pine.

## 3. Webull Desktop, if the gap is execution at zero cost

[Webull Desktop](https://www.webull.com/download) is a brokerage platform, so the trade-off is built in: the charting is free and connected to an order ticket, and it only knows about a Webull account. It runs on Windows and macOS with paper trading, pattern recognition on charts, a TPO indicator, voice quotes, an ETF screener and options tools.

The [fees page](https://www.webull.com/pricing) lists $0 commissions on US-listed stocks, ETFs and options, with a $0.50 per contract fee on certain index options, plus the standard regulatory pass-throughs (FINRA $0.000195 per share on sells, capped at $9.79 a trade). Outgoing stock transfers are $75. Paid market data subscriptions exist but the pages I checked do not list their prices, so I am not quoting one.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** puts the order ticket on the chart, with no platform fee.
**What it lacks:** everything held anywhere other than Webull, a scripting layer, and a community. The charting is good for a broker and short of TradingView on indicators and drawing.

## 4. TradeStation Desktop, if the gap is strategy code and futures

[TradeStation Desktop](https://www.tradestation.com/platforms-and-tools/desktop/) is the platform most people mean when they say "institutional-feeling retail software." RadarScreen scans and monitors up to 1,000 symbols in real time across 180-plus technical and fundamental indicators. EasyLanguage is the proprietary language for building, testing, optimizing and running automated strategies, with decades of historical data behind the backtester. OptionStation Pro handles options chains and position graphs; the Matrix shows market depth. Asset classes are stocks, options, futures and futures options.

On [pricing](https://www.tradestation.com/pricing/), stock commissions are $0 per share. Options list at $0.40 to $0.80 per contract by volume tier, with a limited-time $0 per leg offer for new accounts. There is a $10 a month inactivity fee that is waived when the account meets a minimum activity level. The platform with RadarScreen is priced at $99.99 a month for non-professional subscribers who are not brokerage customers ($199.99 for professionals); the pricing page does not state a separate platform fee for brokerage clients, which is the arrangement the desktop page describes.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** a full strategy language wired to live execution and to futures, with a backtester built for it.
**What it lacks:** simplicity. This is a heavy platform with a learning curve to match, and it charts only what TradeStation trades.

## 5. Koyfin, if the gap is fundamentals and macro

[Koyfin](https://www.koyfin.com/pricing/) is a research dashboard, not a charting tool, and it earns a place here because the most common "TradingView is missing something" complaint from long-term holders is fundamental depth. Financial statements, estimates, macro and rates data, custom dashboards and a screener that goes much deeper than TradingView's on financial history.

The free plan gives 2 years of financials, 1 year of estimates, 2 watchlists, 2 screens and 2 custom dashboards, with limited snapshots and news. Plus is $39 a month and Premium is $79 a month; the page advertises up to 30% off with an annual plan but does not display the annual figure, so I am not quoting one. Advisor Core and Advisor Pro sit above at $209 and $299 a month.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** fundamentals, estimates and macro data at professional depth, laid out in dashboards you design.
**What it lacks:** charting. The charts exist, and they are fine for overlays, but nobody picks Koyfin for drawing tools or alerts. I wrote a fuller comparison in [Best Koyfin Alternatives](/blog/best-koyfin-alternatives).

## 6. Finviz, if the gap is fast screening

[Finviz](https://finviz.com/elite.ashx) is the fastest way to answer "which stocks currently look like X" across the whole US market, and its heatmap is the best free picture of a market day on the internet. The charts are basic. The screener is the product.

The free tier is delayed quotes, 20 rows per page, 50 holdings per portfolio, 3 years of statements, and ads. Elite is $39.50 a month or $299.50 a year with a free trial, and lifts that to real-time data including premarket (4 AM to 9:30 AM) and aftermarket (4 PM to 8 PM), 8 years of statements, 100 rows per page, 500 holdings per portfolio, unlimited email and push alerts, Excel and CSV export, and no ads.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** descriptive screening at speed, and the heatmap.
**What it lacks:** charting worth the name, and any awareness of what you own beyond a typed list. The longer version is in [Best Finviz Alternatives](/blog/best-finviz-alternatives).

## 7. MetaTrader 5, if the gap is algorithmic trading through a broker

[MetaTrader 5](https://www.metatrader5.com/en) is a free download for Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android and the browser, built around MQL5, a programming language for indicators and automated Expert Advisors, and a Strategy Tester for running them against history. The vendor describes it as covering forex, stocks and futures.

The platform costs nothing, but it trades only through a broker that supports it, so in practice you get MT5 by opening an account with one of those brokers, and that list skews toward forex and CFD shops. For US equities specifically, the broker choice is the constraint, not the software.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** free, broker-executed algorithmic trading with a mature strategy tester and a large marketplace of ready-made robots.
**What it lacks:** modern charting ergonomics, and it is only as good as the broker behind it.

## 8. Helm Terminal, if the gap is what happens after you buy

I build Helm Terminal, so weigh this section accordingly. It is not a charting platform and it does not replace TradingView for charts. It is the portfolio layer: the tool that knows what you own, which none of the charting tools above do.

Helm connects to brokerage accounts read-only through Plaid, or takes manually entered positions, and then reports on the whole book: concentration across accounts, upcoming earnings exposure for positions you hold, tax-loss candidates, and one monitored investment thesis on the free tier. The monitoring reads SEC filings and reporting against the reason you own a position and flags when the evidence changes, quoting the source.

Free includes the portfolio dashboard, AI stock analysis, one monitored thesis and the general morning brief. Pro is $20 a month or $149 a year and adds whole-portfolio thesis monitoring, earnings exposure, detailed tax lots and wash-sale screening. The stock analysis at [/analyze](/analyze) needs no account.

**What it does that TradingView does not:** knows your positions across brokerages and watches them.
**What it lacks:** charting, drawing, technical alerts and a screener. If those are the job, this is the wrong tool, and TradingView stays open.

## How the pieces fit

Most people searching for a TradingView alternative are not looking for a replacement. They are looking for the thing TradingView leaves out.

If the gap is price, the free tiers of TradingView and StockCharts overlap heavily, and the broker platforms cost nothing beyond the account. If the gap is automation, TrendSpider and TradeStation are the two serious answers, at very different price points and with very different learning curves. If the gap is execution from the chart, that is what Webull Desktop and TradeStation Desktop exist for. If the gap is fundamentals or screening, that is Koyfin and Finviz, and both pair with TradingView rather than replacing it. If the gap is that your real positions live across three brokerages and nothing is watching them as a whole, that is a different category of tool entirely, described in [How to Track Multiple Brokerage Accounts](/blog/how-to-track-multiple-brokerage-accounts).

A charting tool tells you what price is doing. None of the ones above know what that means for the portfolio you actually hold. That is the line every tool on this list sits on one side of.
