Finviz and TradingView show up in the same search because people comparing them are usually trying to solve two different problems with one tool. Finviz is built to filter the entire market down to a short list. TradingView is built to chart whatever is already on that list, in depth, with alerts and drawing tools attached. They overlap just enough, a screener on each side, a chart on each side, that it looks like a straight substitution. It mostly is not. Figures here were checked against finviz.com and tradingview.com in September 2026; those pages are the source of record if anything here has since changed.

If the plan is to replace one of these outright rather than decide which job each one does, the [Finviz alternatives](/blog/best-finviz-alternatives) and [TradingView alternatives](/blog/best-tradingview-alternatives) posts cover that ground directly.

## What Finviz does best

Finviz's screener is the reason people open it. It filters the US market on fundamental, technical and descriptive criteria at once, sector, market cap, P/E, RSI, insider activity and dozens of other fields, and returns a table in seconds. The heat map, colored by daily performance and sized by market cap, is still one of the fastest ways to read a trading day at a glance without opening a single chart.

The free version of Finviz already covers real screening work. Per [finviz.com/elite.ashx](https://finviz.com/elite.ashx), the free tier includes the core filter set, up to 50 saved screener presets, 20 results per page, and quotes on a roughly 3-minute delay. Elite, at 39.50 dollars a month or 299.50 dollars billed annually (about 24.96 dollars a month), raises those limits: real-time quotes with premarket data from 4:00 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. and after-hours data from 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m., a 10-second auto-refresh instead of 3 minutes, unlimited saved presets, 100 results per page, 8 years of financial statements instead of 3, full ETF holdings instead of a top-10-only view, unlimited email and push alerts, CSV and Excel export, and an interactive charting suite with unlimited intraday charts and 12 available multi-layout views. Portfolio tracking also expands from 50 portfolios with 50 holdings each on the free tier to 100 portfolios with 500 holdings each on Elite.

What Finviz does not do, on either tier, is act as a charting platform in the sense a technical trader means it. There is no deep indicator library, no scripting language, and no community of shared chart templates. Finviz finds the stock. It was never built to be where someone watches it all day.

## What TradingView does best

TradingView's whole product is the chart. Indicators, drawing tools, replay mode, alerts that fire on price or on a custom indicator condition, and a large library of public and paid scripts written by other users are the reasons active traders default to it. Multiple charts and multiple indicators per chart scale directly with the paid tier, which makes the free plan feel noticeably more limited here than Finviz's free plan does in screening.

Per [tradingview.com/pricing](https://www.tradingview.com/pricing/), the free Basic plan is ad-supported and caps a user at 1 chart per tab, 2 indicators per chart, 3 price alerts and 0 server-side technical alerts, with a 30-symbol watchlist and 5,000 bars of historical data. The four paid tiers, billed annually, are Essential at 12.95 dollars a month (saving 24 dollars a year versus paying monthly), Plus at 29.95 dollars a month (saving 60 dollars a year), Premium at 59.95 dollars a month (saving 120 dollars a year), and Ultimate at 199.95 dollars a month (saving 480 dollars a year). Each step up raises chart and indicator limits together: Essential allows 2 charts per tab and 5 indicators per chart with 20 price and 20 technical alerts; Plus allows 4 charts and 10 indicators with 100 alerts of each type; Premium allows 8 charts and 25 indicators with 400 alerts of each type plus 2 watchlist-wide alerts; Ultimate allows 16 charts and 50 indicators with 1,000 alerts of each type and 15 watchlist-wide alerts. All paid tiers remove ads and add access across web, desktop and mobile apps.

TradingView also runs a screener, but it sits underneath the charting product rather than the other way around. It is capable, and free to use with the same account limits as the rest of the free plan, but it is not the reason most people open TradingView first.

## When someone runs both

The two tools are built for adjacent steps rather than the same step, which is why a lot of active investors keep both open. Finviz answers "what should I be looking at," scanning the whole market against a filter in seconds. TradingView answers "what is this one thing doing right now," once a ticker has already cleared that first filter and the question becomes about price action, levels and timing. Someone doing fundamental screening across hundreds of tickers a week gets more from Finviz's free tier alone than from TradingView's free charting limits. Someone who lives on one or two charts all day, watching levels and managing alerts, gets more from a TradingView plan than from Finviz's charting add-on, even on Elite. Running both is not redundant the way it can feel from the search query alone; it is closer to keeping a filing cabinet and a magnifying glass on the same desk.

## Verdict

Finviz and TradingView solve different problems that happen to share a search query. Finviz is the faster, cheaper way to filter the whole market and read it at a glance, with a free tier that covers real work and a single paid tier, Elite, that raises the data and history limits. TradingView is the deeper charting platform, with alerts, indicators and layouts that scale meaningfully across four paid tiers, and a free plan that is functional but tight compared to Finviz's free screener. Someone who mostly screens should start with Finviz and add Elite when the free limits start to bind. Someone who mostly charts should start with TradingView and pick a paid tier based on how many charts and alerts they actually run at once. Someone doing both jobs regularly is the common case, not the edge case, and ends up paying for pieces of each.
